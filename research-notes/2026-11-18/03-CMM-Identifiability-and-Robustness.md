# Identifiability of four-parameter normal-deviation correction under planar and spherical CMM sampling

Dev Kumar; Thien Ho

Version 1.0 - 18 November 2026

## Abstract

The public four-parameter CMM correction model is examined through a nullspace argument, singular-value analysis and a reproducible synthetic contamination experiment. A planar XY circle is rank deficient for out-of-plane translation. At 240 samples, condition numbers are approximately 98.83 for a ±1 degree band, 2.44 for a ±45 degree band and 1.73 for a full sphere. In 100 declared synthetic trials, median parameter error decreases from 0.19668 to 0.03497 micrometres with Huber IRLS. No physical calibration, seven-parameter validation or ISO acceptance claim is made.

## Origin

The original collective metrology study was performed at Arts et Metiers, Lille, Semester 8, 2024. This independent 2026 computational analysis examines the public four-parameter correction model, with synthetic data kept distinct from historical ZEISS observations.

A small residual after correction is not sufficient evidence that geometric parameters have been identified. This note examines the four-parameter normal-deviation model actually present in the public MATLAB pipeline. It asks how the sampling geometry affects rank and conditioning, and how a robust solver behaves under one explicitly defined contamination model. The seven-parameter angular module described in the report is not part of this reproduced implementation.

For a circle in the XY plane with radial probing normals, nzi=0 for every point. The third column of X is therefore exactly zero. The vector (0,0,1,0) lies in the nullspace, and translation normal to the plane cannot be recovered from these observations. A pseudoinverse returns one minimum-norm solution, not evidence that the unobservable displacement is zero. Huber weighting cannot restore a missing column.

For a spherical band, vertical information increases with the spread in nz. All numerical comparisons use 240 deterministic azimuths generated by the golden angle, with z chosen uniformly over the declared band and x,y set by unit-normal geometry. These are designed normal sets; they are not imported CMM measurements. Singular values and numerical rank are computed directly, without forming X transpose X [3].

The ±1 degree band is full rank but weakly sensitive to vertical displacement. The ±45 degree band is substantially better conditioned in the unscaled length-parameter model. A full sphere is a numerical reference, not a claim of mechanical accessibility for the MCG.

The synthetic experiment uses true parameters [1,-2,0.5,0.8] micrometres, Gaussian noise σ=0.15 micrometres, and 12 of 240 randomly selected samples receiving a +3 micrometre increment. The fixed seed is 20261118. Across 100 realizations, median Euclidean parameter error is 0.19668 micrometres for least squares and 0.03497 micrometres for Huber IRLS. This is a result for that contamination design, not a general superiority theorem.

The Huber calculation starts from least squares, estimates scale by 1.4826 times the median absolute deviation, uses c=1.345, and solves weighted least squares for at most 100 iterations. The stopping threshold is 10⁻¹⁰ in parameter-vector norm. A robust estimate can reduce sensitivity to isolated large residuals; it does not correct thermal drift, model misspecification, probe qualification errors or an inconsistent unit convention.

The original report uses a seven-parameter diagnostic equation and states that a full Python implementation is provided. That implementation is absent from the audited public component. Its angular synthetic recovery differs by factors near 99 and 9.9 in two channels. The present study makes no claim to repair or validate those angular estimates. Its contribution is restricted to the four-parameter model with explicit observable and unobservable directions.

Matched forward and reverse residuals must retain point correspondence. Their mean isolates an even component only under a declared directional model; half their difference estimates an odd component. The report uses a full difference for one hysteresis definition while the MATLAB routine uses a half difference. Either convention may be defined, but reported values must state which one is used.

Neither within-run twice-standard-deviation nor a synthetic parameter error is an expanded measurement uncertainty. The GUM framework requires a measurement model and contributions from calibration, thermal effects, repeatability and other relevant sources [4]. No ISO conformity or physical uncertainty is inferred here.

Conclusion. Planar radial probing cannot identify all four parameters. A wider spherical band improves numerical observability in the declared model, and Huber IRLS reduces error in the tested contaminated synthetic setting. Geometry and validation scope should be reported alongside any before/after residual reduction.
